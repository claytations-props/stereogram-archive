How to see the 3D

Pair: the card as it was sold. In a real stereoscope each eye got one half.  Parallel: relax your eyes like you are looking through the screen until the two halves merge into a third image in the middle.  Cross-eye: gently cross your eyes until the halves overlap, best for some people and works big on a screen.  Wiggle: no eye tricks needed, the card flickers between left and right and your brain reads the depth anyway.  Anaglyph: wear red/cyan glasses, red on the left eye. Parallel, Cross-Eye, Wiggle and Anaglyph all use the rectified pair from the VR Parlor, aligned and comfort checked. Original Pair shows the card exactly as it was scanned.

The Collection

The Stereograph Archive gathers public domain stereoscopic photographs, the 3D postcards of the nineteenth century, from the great open collections: the Robert N. Dennis Collection at NYPL, the Library of Congress, the Met, the Getty, and the National Archives. Every item is catalogued with its provenance and license, and every record links back to its source.

The Views

Before cinema, the stereoscope was the window on the world: Niagara in flood, Yosemite before the hotels, a war photographed as it happened, the Moon itself in stereo. Browse by collection or series, work the register, or ask the desk to request an item for you.

Measured for Comfort

Old stereo pairs were never perfectly aligned, and misalignment strains the eyes. Every pair here is measured by our rectification process: the halves are registered to a fraction of a percent, the depth is budgeted, and the condition report on each record shows the numbers. Views that cannot meet the standard are held back, not shipped.
